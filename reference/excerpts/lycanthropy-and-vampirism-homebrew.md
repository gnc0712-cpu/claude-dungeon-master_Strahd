# House Rule: Lycanthropy and Vampirism Curses

**This is homebrew, not an official rule.** Reloaded's citation for PC lycanthropy points to "Player Characters as Lycanthropes" (Monster Manual, p. 207, 2014 numbering) — the player didn't have that page handy, and offered this system as a replacement instead. Adopted as this campaign's house rule for both curses unless the table decides otherwise. Revisit/tweak freely; nothing here is owed to any published source.

Designed so lycanthropy tempts (power now, control later) while vampirism corrupts (power now, at an escalating moral cost) — deliberately different shapes so the two curses don't feel like reskins of each other.

## Curse of Lycanthropy

**Transmission.** A creature damaged by a lycanthrope's bite may contract lycanthropy. At the DM's discretion, the creature makes a DC 15 Constitution save; failure means infection.

**Incubation (1d4 days).** Subtle symptoms only: vivid dreams, increased appetite, heightened hearing/smell, irritability, fast-healing wounds, eyes that catch light strangely. *Remove Curse* ends the infection during this window.

**Established Lycanthropy.** Once the incubation period ends:
- *Keen Senses.* Advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks relying on hearing or smell.
- *Predatory Instinct.* Advantage on Wisdom (Survival) checks to track creatures.
- *Regeneration (while transformed).* Regain 5 HP at the start of each turn if above 0 HP.
- *Natural Weapons (while transformed).* Unarmed strikes become claws (1d6 + STR slashing) or bite (1d8 + STR piercing).
- *Damage Immunity (while transformed).* Immune to bludgeoning/piercing/slashing from nonmagical, non-silvered weapons.

**Transformation triggers (involuntary):** full moon, dropping below half HP, becoming frightened/enraged, or DM's call that instinct overwhelms. On trigger, DC 15 Wisdom save — success retains control, failure hands the character to the DM until the transformation ends (typically 1 hour, until incapacitated, or until dawn, depending on strain).

**Voluntary transformation.** DC 15 Wisdom save at first (failure still transforms, but loses control); after three successful controlled transformations, DC drops to 10; eventually full voluntary control with no check.

**Beast Points.** Gain 1 whenever the character willingly gives in to a violent/animalistic impulse.
- 3+: disadvantage on the Wisdom save against losing control.
- 5+: permanent behavioral changes begin showing.
- 10: effectively a true lycanthrope; may lose the ability to voluntarily suppress the curse.

**Curing it.** *Remove Curse* works before the character fully embraces the curse. Once deeply established, the DM may require a higher-level *Remove Curse*, *Greater Restoration*, a special ritual, killing the lycanthrope that caused the infection, or a dedicated quest.

## Curse of Vampirism

Progressive rather than instant — takes weeks to months to fully manifest.

**Transmission.** A creature bitten by a vampire can contract vampirism: DC 15 Constitution save, failure means the curse takes hold.

**Stage I — The Thirst.** Pale skin, cold extremities, insomnia, sunlight sensitivity, blood fascination, reduced appetite for ordinary food.
- *Darkvision.* 60 ft., or +30 ft. if already possessed.
- *Vampiric Hunger.* Must consume blood at least once every 3 days, or gain a level of exhaustion that persists until blood is consumed.

**Stage II — The Predator.** After feeding several times:
- *Regeneration.* Regain 5 HP at the start of each turn if above 0 HP and not in sunlight/running water.
- *Vampiric Bite.* Melee, STR + proficiency to hit, reach 5 ft. Hit: 1d6 + STR piercing plus 2d6 necrotic; regain HP equal to necrotic damage dealt.
- *Charm of the Blood (1/long rest).* Force a creature within 30 ft. to make a DC 15 Wisdom save or be charmed for 1 minute.

**Stage III — The Curse Revealed.**
- *Spider Climb.*
- *Misty Escape (1/long rest).* When reduced to 0 HP, transform to mist instead and move up to half speed; regain 1 HP after 1 minute if not in sunlight.
- *Undead Resilience.* No longer needs to eat, drink, or breathe.

**Weaknesses** (apply from Stage III on, more severely than Stage I/II):
- *Sunlight.* 10 radiant damage at the start of each turn in direct sunlight, plus disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks while exposed.
- *Running Water.* Can't willingly cross unless carried/transported.
- *Invitation.* Can't willingly enter a private residence without invitation from someone with authority to give it.
- *Hunger.* Escalating consequences the longer blood is denied.
- *Holy Power (DM's discretion).* Radiant damage, consecrated ground, and divine effects may interact especially strongly with the curse.

**Blood Hunger (0–5 track):**

| Hunger | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0 | Recently fed; no penalty |
| 1 | Constant craving |
| 2 | Disadvantage on saves against the temptation to drink blood |
| 3 | Disadvantage on Wisdom checks; visibly predatory |
| 4 | Disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws |
| 5 | DM may force a bloodlust episode |

Drinking a significant amount of blood reduces Hunger by 1d3; from an intelligent humanoid, by 1d4; from a willing creature, normally. Killing specifically to feed can drop Hunger to 0 immediately, but grants a point of **Blood Debt** — a roleplay-facing marker of the character's supernatural/psychological corruption.

**Becoming a true vampire.** At a DM-chosen story point, the character chooses: **reject** the curse (a quest/ritual to permanently remove it) or **embrace** it. On embrace, do *not* hand the player the full Monster Manual vampire statblock (`reference/excerpts/vampire.md`) — that's far too strong for a PC. Use a scaled-down player template instead:

**Player Vampire Template**
- Type: Undead. Darkvision 120 ft. Resistance to necrotic and nonmagical B/P/S.
- Regeneration 5 HP/round (not while exposed to a weakness).
- Bite: 1d8 + STR piercing + 2d6 necrotic.
- Spider Climb. Misty Escape (1/long rest). Charm (1/long rest). Vampiric Hunger (periodic blood needed).
- All weaknesses above apply, and should be played as genuinely costly — that's the point of the curse.
